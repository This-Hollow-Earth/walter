Methodology

Draft copy for the prototype. Final text comes from E5 (THI-99).

The Radar tells procurement and sustainability-risk leads at EU-connected companies which environmental rules, conditions, tools and methods to act on, trial or watch over the next 1–4 quarters, and for which commodity and origin.

Quadrants

RulesRules & Frameworks: regulations, reporting and disclosure standards, due-diligence regimes, voluntary frameworks.
ConditionsEnvironmental Conditions: what is happening in the ecological systems behind supply chains.
IntelligenceData & Intelligence: datasets, geospatial layers, monitoring and alert systems, models, platforms.
ResponsesResponses & Methods: methods, practices and approaches organisations can apply.

Rings

A ring is a recommended action for the next 1–4 quarters. It never says how severe something is.

ADOPTBuild it into normal practice now. If you haven't, you're behind.
TRIALTest it this year in a bounded pilot with a learning goal.
ASSESSBrief the team, track it and set a decision point. No implementation budget yet.
CAUTIONDon't use it as a primary decision input. Challenge it, corroborate it, or phase it out.

For Conditions, ADOPT means: factor it into sourcing decisions now.

Evidence levels

E1Emerging
E2Corroborated
E3Operational
E4Established
E5Institutionalised

Only Conditions blips carry a severity (Low / Medium / High). There are no 0–100 scores anywhere.

Editorial method

  • Every blip has at least 2 sources. E3 or higher needs at least 1 primary source. ADOPT needs E3 or higher. CAUTION needs a concrete, sourced reason.
  • Every ring change comes with a one-line reason that names its evidence.
  • A blip is an entity (a rule, condition, tool or method) with a lifecycle. Events such as delays, releases or retirements are recorded on the blip and can move it between rings.
